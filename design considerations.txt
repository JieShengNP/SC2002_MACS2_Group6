1. Customers served in the end-of-turn summary means customers who have paid. Served customers who are eating or waiting to pay are excluded. The same paid population determines the victory count and satisfaction average. No paid customers is displayed as N/A.

2. Stages 2 and 3 replace the turn-1 Regular arrival with a Critic and the turn-4 arrival with a VIP. Arrivals remain on turns 1, 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16. Stage 1 contains only Regular customers.

3. A Regular loses 8 satisfaction per unserved turn, a VIP loses 5 and a Critic loses 12. A Critic leaves if still unserved and satisfaction is below 50 (inclusive) after the turn-end deduction. At 51 the Critic remains. Other customers leave at zero. Departures cancel the outstanding order, remove the waiting entry or release the table, and add no revenue.

4. Stage 3 uses a 25% combo probability. With probability 75%, a single base item is selected. Combo preparation units are summed. Combo price is 90% of the component total. 

5. VIP discount is applied first, followed by the Happy Hour. Price is locked when the order is taken. A VIP Burger during Happy Hour costs 1500 x 0.9 x 0.8 = 1080 cents, displayed as $10.80. 

6. Happy Hour can be activated once at turn start and lasts for that turn and the next. During each active turn, WAITING, SEATED and ORDERED customers gain 15 satisfaction, capped at 100. This includes customers whose food is READY but has not been served. SERVED, EATING, READY_TO_PAY, PAID and LEFT customers are excluded. Existing order prices remain fixed.
- Activating Happy Hour when several eligible customers have low satisfaction provides greater recovery, while those already at 100 gain nothing. However, new orders receive a 20% discount, reducing revenue per order. The player must therefore balance satisfaction recovery against the revenue target when choosing when to activate it.

7. The Host acts before the Waiter in Stage 3. This lets the Host seat a customer and the Waiter take that customer's order in the same turn. The Waiter still retains the option to seat. Each staff object gets one performTask call per turn, and the input loops allow retries without spending a task.

8. The Waiter acts before the Chef, so food completed by the Chef can be served on the next turn the earliest. A customer served in turn t transitions to EATING at its end, READY_TO_PAY at the end of t+1, and can be charged in t+2. The Cashier runs before new READY_TO_PAY customers join the FIFO payment queue. This preserves a full eating turn and prevents same-turn payment.

9.We require two distinct items so that a ComboMeal offers variety and takes only two to three preparation units. Allowing duplicates would offer more combinations, but two Pastas would increase the Chef’s workload, while two Salads would generate less revenue for the same preparation time as Salad and Burger. We therefore trade some flexibility for manageable preparation time and better revenue potential.

10. We initially used a FIFO queue but changed to a list so managers could choose whom to seat. This allows them to prioritise customers with higher satisfaction to improve the average among paid customers, as departures incur no direct penalty. The trade-off is reduced first-come, first-served fairness and potentially fewer paying customers.
